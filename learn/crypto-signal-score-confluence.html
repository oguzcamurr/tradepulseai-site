How to read a crypto signal score

Probability scores, composite scores and confluence: what a signal's number can and can't tell you.

TradePulse AI guides · Published 24 October 2026

Many crypto apps and channels attach a number to their signals: a score out of 100, a "confidence" percentage, a star rating. These numbers look precise, but they can mean very different things. This guide explains the common types of signal score, what "confluence" means, and the questions worth asking before you give any score weight.

What a trading signal is

A trading signal is the output of a rule or model applied to market data. A simple example: "label the coin BUY when the 7-period moving average is above the 25-period moving average and volume is above its recent average." The output is usually a direction (buy, sell, or no action) plus some supporting information. A signal describes what the rules see in the data right now. It is not knowledge of what price will do next.

Three kinds of signal score

1. Probability-style scores

Some scores claim to be a probability, for example "72% chance this trade hits its target." For a number like that to mean anything, it has to be calibrated: across many past signals that were given 72%, about 72% should have hit the target, measured on data the model was not built on, and after realistic costs. Without published evidence of that kind, a percentage is just a number formatted to look like a probability.

2. Composite or agreement scores

A composite score combines several inputs into one number. Each input (for example trend, momentum, volatility and liquidity) is converted to a common scale, given a weight, and added up. A high composite score means many inputs line up with the conditions the model looks for. It says nothing directly about the chance of profit.

3. "Confidence" labels

Words like "strong", "high confidence" or "premium" are often attached to one of the two types above. The word itself adds no information. What matters is how the number behind it is built and whether it has been tested.

What confluence means

Confluence is the idea that a setup is more interesting when several independent pieces of evidence point the same way: for example, an uptrend on the higher timeframe, momentum turning up on the lower timeframe, and rising trading volume. Composite scores are a way of putting a number on confluence.

The catch is the word independent. Most popular indicators, including RSI, MACD and moving averages, are calculated from the same closing prices. When the price has risen for several candles, all of them tend to turn positive together. "Five indicators agree" can be closer to one observation counted five times. Inputs built from different data, such as volume or the range of each candle, add more genuinely separate information than another price-based oscillator.

Questions to ask about any signal score

  1. What are the inputs? If the provider won't say what goes into the score at all, you can't judge what it measures.
  2. What does the scale mean? Is it a probability, a weighted sum, or a ranking? Is 80 twice as good as 40, or just higher?
  3. Is the direction label separate from the score? In many systems, the BUY/SELL label and the score come from different rules. A high score does not necessarily mean a strong BUY.
  4. Is there a track record, and what kind? Results from live, executed trades are different from simulated or back-tested results. Simulated results usually leave out fees, slippage and the effect of real orders on price.
  5. Which timeframe? A score calculated on 1-hour candles describes a much shorter horizon than one on daily candles.
  6. Is anyone promising returns? US regulators have warned that "AI" trading tools promising high or guaranteed returns are a common feature of fraud. See the CFTC and SEC advisories in the sources below.

How to use a score sensibly

Treat a score as a summary of current conditions, not as a forecast. Look at the individual inputs behind it, since a middling score can hide one input that matters a lot to you, such as very low liquidity. Compare the score with the broader market regime and with volatility and liquidity. Above all, any decision about risk, position size and exits stays with you.
